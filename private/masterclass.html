01 · Mental model

Five layers: hiring an AI employee

MODELRaw intelligence. Reasons, writes, produces code. Same for everyone. Not a competitive advantage.
CONTEXTMemory and background. Your company, your product, your past decisions, your jargon. → This is where the real difference lives.
TOOLSHands. Reads/writes files, runs code, searches the web, drives a browser.
SKILLSTraining / SOPs. "This is how we do this job at our company." Repeatable expertise.
MCPAccess badge. Asana, Gmail, Drive, databases, Obsidian — connections to live systems.

If you only use the MODEL, you've hired a genius and asked questions without ever giving them a desk.

01 · Mental model

Decision tree: when to use what?

ONE-OFF
Just write a good prompt. An email, a summary, a translation. Build no infrastructure. 70% of work ends here.
REPEATED + RULED
Write a skill. If you do it 3+ times a month and there's a "this is how we do it here" part. E.g.: TDS review, quote format.
NEEDS LIVE DATA
Connect an MCP. When the answer depends on current data in your systems. E.g.: "which projects slipped this week?"
MULTI-STEP
Build an agent / subagents. 10+ steps, multiple sources, parallel research. E.g.: competitor landscape report.
IRREVERSIBLE
Human approval required. Outgoing email, customer pricing, formulation changes, signatures. Do not automate.
02 · Anatomy

What does a skill look like?

--- (frontmatter: metadata)
name: tds-review
description: Reviews competitor or own
  TDS/SDS documents against our company
  standard. Trigger: "check this TDS",
  "review data sheet", "competitor product
  analysis", or when a TDS .pdf is uploaded.
---

# TDS Review Procedure

## Always check
1. Binder type and ratio
2. Claimed EN 12004 / EN 1504 class
3. Open time, slip resistance
4. Consistency of claims vs test reports

## Output format
Comparison table + 3 risky claims
+ difference vs our product → one page

## Never do
- Invent test data. If missing, write "NO DATA".
  • One folder, one markdown file. No code, no installation.
  • description = the trigger. This decides when it activates. Spend the most effort here.
  • The body = your procedure. Writing down the "this is how we do it" in your head.
  • You can add templates, checklists, example files alongside.

Critical

A skill's value isn't in its length — it's in containing institutional knowledge that exists nowhere else. If you write generic knowledge, the skill is pointless — the model already knows it.

02 · Anatomy

The real skill: context engineering

  • Give inputs, not roles. "You are an expert" does nothing. Give the real TDS, the real number, the real email.
  • Write the success criteria. "Make it good" isn't measurable. "One page, 5 bullets, a source for every claim" is.
  • State the limits. Writing what it must not do matters as much as what it must do.
  • Give an example. One "good" example beats three paragraphs of explanation.
  • Keep the context clean. The model drifts in long conversations. Start fresh, carry the summary.
  • Plan for iteration. The first output is a draft. "Change this" is faster than rewriting from scratch.

80% of bad output comes from missing context, not from model weakness. When you dislike a result, your first question should be "what information didn't I provide?"

Done by hand

45–60 minutes. Wandering Asana tab by tab, taking notes. And it gets skipped 2 days a week.

Automated

0 minutes. The scheduled task runs at 08:00. You just read and decide.

The critical design decision: the output must be "what you should do today", not "what happened". A system that produces reports produces garbage; a system that produces decisions saves time.

03 · 10:00 — R&D

Literature + patent scan

  • Input: "Shrinkage control in fast-setting cementitious repair mortars — last 5 years, patents + papers."
  • Process: OpenAlex + Google Patents + Espacenet scanned in parallel; results deduplicated and ranked by relevance.
  • Output: Scored list + mechanism summary + a "what it means for us" read + prior-art risk.
  • Archive: Every source lands in Obsidian as a note, linked to current projects.

Watch out here

  • Verify every reference. Don't trust citations without a DOI.
  • The model can invent a "plausible-looking" source. The most dangerous error type — because it looks right.
  • Rule: a link that won't open = doesn't exist.

The real gain

Scan time: 2 days → 20 minutes. But the real gain isn't speed — it's coverage. By hand you scan 15 papers; the system scans 400 and picks the 15.

03 · 18:00 — Institutional memory

Where compound interest kicks in

The model forgets every conversation. That's not a flaw, it's the architecture. Memory is your responsibility.

01The conversation ends
→
02Archived as keyworded MD
03Linked to earlier notes
→
04Becomes a source for the next question

Why this matters more than anything

  • Month 1: You re-explain the context of every question.
  • Month 3: "What did we say about this formulation last quarter?" — the answer comes from the archive.
  • Month 6: The system remembers your decision history better than you do. It shows you your inconsistencies.

A team that doesn't archive starts from zero every day. This is the single highest-return habit.

LAB 2 · 16 minutes

Write your own skill

Pick: a job you do 3+ times a month

Meeting-note formatTDS reviewComplaint triageExperiment report templateSample request evaluationSupplier quote comparison
  • Step 1 · 4 min — Write down how you do this job, as bullets. The checklist in your head.
  • Step 2 · 4 min — Write the trigger phrases. Which words would you use to ask for this?

---
name: meeting-notes
description: Turns a meeting recording or raw
  notes into our meeting format.
  Trigger: "clean up the meeting notes",
  "extract the actions", raw notes pasted.
---

## Format
1. DECISIONS — each one a single sentence
2. ACTIONS — who / what / when (date mandatory)
3. OPEN — unresolved questions
4. RISKS — discussed but ownerless topics

## Rules
- Action with no owner → write "NO OWNER"
- Action with no date → write "NO DATE"
- Don't summarize the chatter. Decisions and actions only.
- Length: never past half a page.

15 lines. The value isn't the length — it's the "NO OWNER" rule.

05 · Risk 01

Fabrication: the most dangerous kind looks correct

Where it happens

  • References, DOIs, patent numbers
  • Standard numbers and clause references (like EN 12004-2 clause 8.3)
  • Competitor product specifications
  • Numeric data — especially in tables, where it looks trustworthy
  • Dates and regulatory status (model memory ages)

The model isn't lying. It doesn't know it's unsure. The difference matters — because it says wrong things in a convincing tone.

Team protocol

  • A link that won't open = doesn't exist. Delete it.
  • Every number needs a source. Unsourced numbers stay in drafts, never go out.
  • Standard claims → the original text. Always.
  • Every outgoing document → a human reads it. No exceptions.
  • Add to your briefs: "If unsure, write 'I DON'T KNOW'." — it works.
05 · Risk 02 & 04

Data boundaries and permission creep

RED LINES
  • Unapproved formulation details
  • Customer contracts, price lists
  • Personal data (under data-protection law)
  • Invention text before a patent filing
  • Third parties' confidential data

Rule: Until corporate policy is settled, ask "would I email this to an outside consultant?" If the answer is no, don't paste it.

PERMISSION CREEP

MCPs also take write access. Read errors are cheap; write errors corrupt real data.

  • New connection → start read-only
  • Grant write access when the need is proven
  • Email sending never automatic — it drafts, you send
  • Bulk delete / bulk update → always with approval
  • Keep a record of who is connected to what
05 · Risk 03

Automation debt

Spending 3 hours automating a 3-minute job — and then doing that job once a month.

The threshold formula

(min × yearly frequency) > setup × 3

If it doesn't pay back the setup cost 3× within the first year, don't automate it. Do it by hand.

JobYearlyCall
Morning briefing250×45minBUILD
Monthly report format12×30minBUILD
Annual budget template1×2hBY HAND

The other two hidden costs

Maintenance debt

Every flow you build is a breakable thing. An Asana field gets renamed, the flow goes silent. A system that silently runs wrong is worse than one that doesn't run.

Skill atrophy

If you never do the literature scan yourself, you can't tell when the system does a bad one. Do it by hand occasionally — for calibration.

05 · Limits

Never delegated

Final technical approval. Formulation changes, safety declarations, standards compliance.
Pricing and commercial commitments. Every number that goes to a customer.
People decisions. Hiring, performance, promotion. Input, yes; decision, no.
Legal and regulatory declarations. REACH, biocidal, CE. Drafts yes, signatures no.
Crisis communication. Customer complaints, field problems, press.
Strategic direction. Deciding which problem is worth solving — that's your job. The hardest and most valuable part.

Cognitive work gets delegated. Responsibility doesn't. You sign the signature.
